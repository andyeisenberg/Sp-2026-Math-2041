    Worksheet Autonomous Equations
    

    A first-order autonomous equation has the form
    \begin{gather*} \frac{dy}{dt} = f(y). \end{gather*}
    In class, we introduced the phase diagram. In this recitation, you should think carefully about the relationship between the graph of \(f(y)\text{,}\) the phase diagram of the differential equation, and the classification of the equalibrium points as stable, unstable, or semistable.
    🔗

    1.

    Consider the equation \(\frac{dy}{dt} = (y - 1)(y - 2)^2(y - 3)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Sketch the graph of \(f(y)\text{.}\) Your sketch should have the correct zeros of the function, and should correctly indicate where \(f(y)\) is positive and negative. (Note: the horizontal axis should be \(y\) here. We don’t have a label for the vertical axis.)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Draw the \(ty\)-plane, which is where solution functions should be graphed. Draw the equilibrium solutions. Along the \(y\)-axis, put appropriate up- and down-arrows, and complete the phase diagram. Classify each equilibrium point as stable, unstable, or semistable.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Describe how the graph of \(f(y)\) could give the up- and down-arrow phase diagram information. What features in the graph of \(f(y)\) can you recognize as indications of stable, unstable, or semistable equilibrium solutions?
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    One model of population growth is:
    \begin{gather*} \frac{dP}{dt} = f(P) = rP\left(\frac{P}{T} - 1\right)\left(1 - \frac{P}{K}\right), \end{gather*}
    where \(r, K, T\) are positive constants with \(T \lt K\text{.}\) This model is meant to capture the idea that there is some threshold \(T\) below which the population can’t sustain itself, and there is a carrying capacity \(K\) above which the environment doesn’t have sufficient resources for the population.
    🔗

    (a)

    Sketch a graph of \(f(P)\text{.}\) Without drawing a phase diagram, identify the equilibrium solutions to the differential equation and classify them as stable, unstable, or semistable.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Draw the phase diagram and compare to your graph of \(f(P)\text{.}\)
